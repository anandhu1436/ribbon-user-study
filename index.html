WELCOME TO THE RIBBON PATTERN STUDY

How should a sketch change when its shape changes?

Imagine a tube drawn with red ribbon-like strokes. When the tube is shortened, stretched, squeezed, or bent, what should happen to those strokes? We want to understand which patterns look natural to you.

STAGE 1 · 16 COMPARISONS

Choose the result you prefer

Look at the original pattern and two results labelled A and B. Pick the result you like better, or choose No preference. The order of the options is randomized.

STAGE 2 · 4 DRAWINGS

Draw your expected pattern

Each reference has just 6–8 ribbons. Draw on the changed tube using a mouse, pen, or touch. You may add, remove, or reposition ribbons. Erase and undo are available.

There are no right or wrong answers

No artistic skill is required. Follow your own preference; explanations and confidence ratings are optional. You can end the session early.

Shape, spacing & ribbon identity

These patterns follow your red reference: one coherent stroke direction, imperfect spacing, and thick 2D ribbon strips. The cylinder outline is a guide, not a crosshatched surface.

Pattern lab

Try shortening, lengthening, squeezing, or bending. “Deform only” carries all ribbons with the surface. “Adapt pattern” inserts or removes ribbons within matching pattern families when their perpendicular spacing changes. This is an illustrative heuristic, not a validated perceptual model or the RibbonSculpt ARAP solver.

Drawing study

Four patterns × four deformations, shuffled for each session. Participants see the original and draw on a blank target, without algorithmic suggestions. The study uses the bent cylinder and a fixed ribbon width. No response is labelled correct.
